Chencharu Central Residences / Location

Location

Khatib is one stop from Yishun. The city is further than some guides say.

The site is at Chencharu Close, Yishun, District 27. The nearest MRT named by every serious source is Khatib, NS14, on the North-South Line.

By Jack Leow, CEA R074023ILast checked 18 November 2026

Stops you can count

Station numbers below are the North-South Line codes. Orchard is NS22, so Khatib to Orchard is 8 stops. Raffles Place is NS26, so Khatib to Raffles Place is 12 stops. The PropNex ebook says 13. This guide uses 12. Confirmed

  1. NS13Yishun, one stop north. Northpoint City is at this station. Confirmed
  2. NS14Khatib. The station this site is described against. Confirmed
  3. NS22Orchard, 8 stops south. Confirmed
  4. NS26Raffles Place, 12 stops south. Confirmed

The walk itself is not a surveyed distance. Gamuda’s 29 September 2025 filing says the site is within 450 metres of Khatib MRT. Confirmed The ebook says a fully sheltered walk of under 6 minutes. Another project site says about 330 to 350 metres. A listing has used about 460 metres. This guide did not measure the path, and it does not turn the minutes into metres. The sheltered link is part of a project that is not built. Reported

The North-South Corridor has moved

The ebook says the corridor opens from 2029 and aims to cut northern journeys by up to 30 minutes. That is not the timeline LTA published in September 2026.

  • The viaduct from Admiralty Road West to Lentor Avenue is now expected in phases from 2029, not 2027. Confirmed
  • The tunnel from Lentor Avenue to the East Coast Parkway is now expected in phases from 2031, not 2029. The city-bound section is this tunnel. Confirmed
  • This guide does not repeat a 30-minute saving. LTA’s September 2026 notices used here do not state that figure.

Yishun already has the North-South Line. The corridor is a future road project, not the way residents reach Khatib MRT.

The town around the site

The Business Times, quoting PropNex research at the tender, named Peiying Primary, Chung Cheng High (Yishun), Naval Base Primary, Naval Base Secondary, Orchid Park Secondary and Yishun Innova Junior College as schools near the plot. It also named Khatib Polyclinic, Yishun Stadium and Sports Hall, and Lower Seletar Reservoir Park. None of those was given as a measured walk. Confirmed

The ebook adds Northland Primary, HomeTeamNS Khatib, and a “minutes walk” to Lower Seletar Reservoir and the Khatib Bongsu mangroves. Those minutes are not used here as distances. Reported

Chencharu is a new precinct inside Yishun. HDB’s 12 June 2024 plan, reported by CNA and The Business Times, is about 70 hectares bounded by Yishun Avenue 1, Yishun Avenue 2 and Sembawang Road, about 10,000 homes by 2040, and at least 80% of them public housing. Gamuda’s filing repeats about 10,000 homes by 2040. Confirmed The ebook’s “fewer than 2,000 private homes” is tighter than “at least 80% public”, which allows up to about 2,000 private homes. This guide does not treat the two sentences as the same claim.

HDB said Chencharu Link, running north-south through the estate, is a 400-metre bus-only road. A community avenue runs east-west from a new park to the existing neighbourhood park near Khatib. The bungalow at 50 Bah Soon Pah Road, a plantation house about 100 years old and likely built in the early 1910s by the Bukit Sembawang Rubber Company, is to be adapted for community use inside that park, with a heritage trail, gardens and storyboards. Cycling links are planned toward Sembawang and Lower Seletar Reservoir. Confirmed

HDB also said Ground-Up Initiative and the Live Turtle and Tortoise Museum will move to a site next to HomeTeamNS Khatib. That is a plan to relocate them. It is not a statement that they have already moved. Confirmed

Mothership, citing that same HDB release, adds a planned educational institution and a nursing home. CNA’s account of the day does not. Reported

Public flats already launched are a separate count from this private project. HDB said in its 11 December 2025 gazette notice that four BTO projects had been launched, with about three applications for each available flat. The first, announced in June 2024, was about 1,270 homes. The Business Times put the two February 2025 projects, Chencharu Vines and Chencharu Green, at 1,531 flats together. Confirmed

Project pages put the October 2025 launch, Chencharu Grove, at 826 flats. Reported HDB then launched a fifth project, Chencharu Acres, in the November 2026 sales exercise. CNA reported that launch on 4 November 2026. Confirmed Write-ups of Chencharu Acres put it at 1,582 flats. Reported

The ebook’s “about 5,200 flats from five BTO projects” is not used as a total. The launches are now five, and the counts above are not added into one figure here. Reported

On 11 December 2025 HDB published an environmental study of a vegetated patch of about 7 hectares along Yishun Avenue 1, south of Yishun Walk. The study found no area of high ecological value. HDB said about 60% of the patch is scrubland and urban vegetation. The straw-headed bulbul was among the species recorded. Feedback ran to 7 January 2026. Confirmed The Straits Times, citing the report, said about 40% is forest on abandoned plantation land. Confirmed The report expects clearance to start in the third quarter of 2026, after an environmental management plan and approvals. The Straits Times described that clearance as late 2026. Reported
